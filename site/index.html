No ads, no account, no clutter. PipeTV is a free and open source app for Android TV and Google TV.

Why PipeTV

Everything is one press of the remote away, and nothing stands between you and the video.

Made for the remote

Left opens the side rail, Up and Down move along it, OK goes there. A soft white outline glides to whatever has the focus, so you always know where you are.

Straight to the video

Choose a video and it plays fullscreen right away. Press Down while it plays and the related videos come up over it, as in YouTube's own TV app. When it ends, the next one starts after a countdown you can cancel.

A home of rows

What you watched recently, more like the last of it, what's live, and what's new from your subscriptions, as soon as PipeTV opens.

Light on small sticks

Made and measured on a Mi TV Stick with 1 GB of memory, where it opens in 2 to 3 seconds.

Private

No ads, no account, no tracking. Your subscriptions, history and playlists stay on your TV, and SponsorBlock skips sponsored segments.

Updates itself

A new version shows up at the top of the home. Choose it, see what's new, and PipeTV downloads it, checks it and hands it to Android's installer.

And it's yours to tune: hide shorts, paid and upcoming videos, filter out keywords or channels, choose how far Left and Right seek, or turn on DeArrow for crowdsourced titles and thumbnails.

Install

PipeTV runs on TVs, sticks and boxes with Android TV or Google TV, Android 6.0 or newer. It installs next to YouTube and PipePipe, without replacing them.

  1. Get Downloader on the TV

    Install Downloader from the Play Store on your TV. It's free.

  2. Open PipeTV's address in it

    Type felipebueno.github.io/PipeTV and choose Download for Android TV, or type the APK's address directly:

    https://github.com/felipebueno/PipeTV/releases/latest/download/PipeTV-armeabi-v7a.apk
  3. Install

    When the TV asks, allow Downloader to install unknown apps, then choose Install and Open.

Which APK?

PipeTV-armeabi-v7a.apk runs on nearly every TV, stick and box, even those with 64-bit chips that run a 32-bit Android, like the Mi TV Stick and the Chromecast with Google TV.

TVs with a 64-bit Android, like the NVIDIA Shield TV, can take PipeTV-arm64-v8a.apk. All of them are on the latest release.

Other ways

From a computer on the same network, with the TV's developer options on: adb connect <the TV's IP address>, then adb install PipeTV-armeabi-v7a.apk.

Or copy the APK to a USB drive and open it with a file manager on the TV.

Updates

PipeTV looks for new versions as you use it, at most once an hour, and offers them at the top of its home. Choose Update, confirm in Android's installer, then Open.

Your history and subscriptions stay as they are.

Built on PipePipe

PipeTV is a fork of PipePipe, which grew out of NewPipe. They do the hard work of talking to YouTube; PipeTV reshapes the app for the TV and keeps up with their updates. On a phone or tablet, PipeTV behaves like PipePipe.

It's free software under the GNU General Public License v3.0, and its code is on GitHub, where the changelog tells what each version brought.
